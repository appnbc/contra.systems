Tactile Curation: Engineering Play, Materiality, and Social Connection in Spatial Computing

01

Artist Statement

Interactive environments and digital architectures shape how people move, behave, and connect when they share physical space. My practice brings the hidden rules of computation into the room through spatial audio, computer vision, and tactile materials. Growing up around my parents' craft as jewelers taught me that technical precision matters most when it serves the human body, a material sensibility I bring to spatial computing and interactive installation design. Grounded in my experience across conversational AI, UX strategy, and game studies, alongside my perspective as a gay Guatemalan American, I examine how designed systems govern participation and cultural interpretation. Across my work, from robotics and wearable spatial audio to my current residency at Accademia Unidee (Cittadellarte, Fondazione Pistoletto) in Biella, Italy, where I weave raw sheep's wool with programmable LED matrices, I build multisensory spaces that act as spatial games. As visitors walk through these installations, the architecture reacts to their proximity and movement, inviting strangers to touch, collaborate, and negotiate shared space together.

02

OF(F) THE GAZE: Machine Perception as Spatial Encounter ↗

Role
Concept lead and installation architect
Format
Robotic installation and spatial visualization
Context
Ars Electronica Festival, Linz, Austria, 2023

OF(F) THE GAZE stages machine vision as a shared physical encounter. A Boston Dynamics Spot robot navigates an overhead canopy of tensioned reflective tiles, folding the shifting reflections of visitors directly into the robot's camera feeds. By placing human bodies and autonomous robotics inside the same mirrored environment, the installation prompts strangers to coordinate their movements, observe one another in the overhead grid, and collectively test the boundaries of how an automated system tracks group presence.

04

Kynd: Familiarity, Orientation, and Spatial Audio ↗

Format
Augmented-reality prototype and spatial audio interaction
Context
MIT Reality Hack, Cambridge, MA, 2026; Honorable Mention, Community Hack

Kynd explores how augmented reality and directional spatial audio can support spatial orientation for people navigating memory loss. Built on smart glasses, the prototype pairs computer vision with localized audio whispers that deliver familiar voices and context cues within the user's immediate surroundings. Treating acoustic timing and spatial placement as primary design materials allows the system to guide physical movement and preserve human agency in everyday environments.

Re-CREATE: Participatory Environmental Modeling ↗

Format
Participatory installation and interactive environmental modeling
Context
Art and Science Symposium, Aalborg University, Denmark, 2023

Re-CREATE turns the gallery into a collaborative studio where visitors arrange physical objects inside a light-controlled miniature box to design shared environments. Participants pair their physical arrangements with an AI-generated background and custom programmable LED lighting before photographing their room to join a collective digital house. Combining hands-on miniature making with responsive lighting turns environmental design into a playful, social activity across gallery visitors.

08

Language and Platforms: Scaling Linguistic and Educational Infrastructure

At Amazon Alexa, I built localization workflows for the platform's inaugural Spanish and Italian launches, mapping regional colloquialisms to standardized intent categories for natural language understanding. At Vista Higher Learning, I served as a Developmental Editor for European Languages, guiding the transformation of print language textbooks into interactive virtual learning platforms. Combined with my work directing bilingual voice-interface design at Versay Solutions and researching AI platform governance at Red Trig, these roles revealed how linguistic taxonomies and interface rules determine how people learn, speak, and participate across digital systems. This foundation directly informs how I design inclusive, legible rules for interactive physical spaces.

09

Platforms and Play: Ludological Rulesets and Spatial Interaction

My Erasmus Mundus Joint Master's thesis, Pokémon Phenomenon: Game Mechanics and its Effect on Narratives, analyzed how interactive rules and game mechanics steer storytelling and social exchange across the Pokémon media universe. By examining how location-locked assets and structural rules guide player behavior and community collaboration, this research establishes the theoretical foundation for my current practice: treating room-scale installations as spatial games where aesthetics, sensors, and physical boundaries invite people to play and connect.

11

Coda

I study computation through the human encounters it shapes: walking into a room, hearing a familiar voice, touching a luminous textile, and coordinating movement with a stranger. By turning digital rules into tactile, sonic, and spatial experiences, my work explores how immersive environments can foster curiosity, wonder, and shared human connection.
